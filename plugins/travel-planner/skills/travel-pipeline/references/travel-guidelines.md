# Travel Planning Guidelines

Reference rules for every phase of the pipeline. These are hard lessons, not suggestions.

---

## Research quality rules (Phase 1)

- **Timing data requires 3 sources.** If you can't find 3 traveler accounts confirming a crowd pattern
  (e.g. "Fushimi Inari is empty before 7am"), flag it as unconfirmed.
- **Hours and prices change.** Always search for the current year. An attraction's "free entry" might
  have changed to paid admission.
- **Reddit > tourism blogs.** `r/[CityTravel]` and `r/solotravel` have real on-the-ground intel.
  Tourism boards have marketing copy. Weight accordingly.
- **Book-in-advance table is mandatory.** Missing a sell-out attraction is the #1 trip regret.
  Always include lead times and booking links.

---

## Itinerary quality rules (Phase 2)

- **One zone per day.** Never send travelers across the city twice. This is the single biggest
  optimization a planner can make.
- **Realistic timing.** A 30-minute metro ride + 15-minute walk + museum check-in = 1 hour, not 30 minutes.
  Always add 20-min buffers between stops.
- **Last day = light schedule.** The day before departure needs packing time, checkout, bag storage, and
  airport transit buffer.
- **Transit specifics are non-negotiable.** "Take the metro" is not output. Line name + origin stop +
  destination stop + minutes + cost. Every time.
- **Meal times must match local culture.** In Spain, lunch at 2pm and dinner at 9pm. In Japan, dinner at 6pm.
  Scheduling lunch at noon in Madrid means eating at tourist traps.
- **Anchor logistics in the itinerary.** Hotel check-out at 11:00 with a 09:30 train means bags at
  reception at 08:30, not a 10:00 "morning sightseeing" block.

---

## Budget rules (Phase 3)

- **Never underestimate food.** A "mid-range" food budget should include: real breakfast, sit-down lunch,
  sit-down dinner, plus daily snacks/street food/desserts/coffee. Most planners undercount by 30%.
- **3 shopping tiers is mandatory.** The user decides how much they'll shop — not the planner.
  Present light / normal / intense scenarios with amounts per day.
- **Cash vs card guidance required.** Some destinations are cash-heavy (Japan, rural areas).
  Tell the user how much cash to arrive with vs rely on cards.
- **Separate "already paid" clearly.** Travelers hate being told to budget for something they already paid.
  List paid items as "pagado ✓" and exclude them from the "bring this much" total.
- **Exchange rates: label them.** Always note the date of the rate and tell the user to verify before travel.

---

## HTML guide rules (Phase 4)

- **Photos must match the visit time of day.** A night photo of the Skytree for a night visit. Dawn at
  Fushimi Inari. This is what makes the guide inspiring, not just informative.
- **All images base64-embedded.** No external URLs — they break in airplane mode.
- **Self-contained = offline-ready.** The guide must work with no internet. The traveler will use it
  in the street, on the train, at the temple. Test that assumption.
- **Emergency info on its own page.** Embassy phone, local emergency numbers, nearest hospital.
  This page should be findable in 5 seconds.

## Traveler profile quick reference

What to ask when building a traveler profile from scratch:

1. **Travel style**: adventurous / cultural / relaxed / family / digital nomad
2. **Budget level**: budget / mid-range / luxury
3. **Pace**: packed (every hour scheduled) / moderate / relaxed (spontaneous)
4. **Accommodation**: hostel / hotel / boutique / Airbnb / resort
5. **Interests** (pick top 5): culture, food, hiking, photography, nightlife, nature, shopping, wellness,
   history, architecture, beaches, adventure sports, local markets
6. **Dietary restrictions**: vegetarian, vegan, gluten-free, halal, kosher, allergies
7. **Companions**: solo, couple, family with kids (ages?), group of friends
8. **Languages**: what languages do you speak beyond Spanish?
9. **Experience level**: first international trip / seasoned traveler / expert
10. **Bucket list destinations**: 3-5 places you've always wanted to go and why
11. **Deal-breakers**: very long flights? extreme heat? visa hassle? heavy crowds?
